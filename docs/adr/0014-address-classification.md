# Address classification: bogons and global addresses

`isBogon` and `isGlobal` follow the "Globally Reachable" column of IANA's IPv4 and IPv6 special-purpose address registries, as of the 2025-10-09 snapshot. A block marked False is a bogon. `isBogon` is the union of named predicates per family (loopback, private, link-local, documentation and so on), so every such block has one; a reachable allocation inside it, such as `192.0.0.9` in `192.0.0.0/24` or `2001:20::/28` in `2001::/23`, is carved out of the predicate. A block marked True, such as `64:ff9b::/96`, is global. Libnet also counts multicast and the deprecated blocks, IPv6 site-local and ORCHID and the IPv4 6to4 relay anycast block, as bogons.

A block marked N/A is a transition form: neither a bogon nor global. For IPv6 these are 6to4 and Teredo, and libnet treats IPv4-mapped and IPv4-compatible addresses the same way: they are routable but are not native global unicast. IPv4 has no transition forms, so `ipv4.isGlobal` is exactly `!isBogon`, while `ipv6.isGlobal` also excludes the four transition forms.

`registry.bogons` lists the same space as CIDR literals for iteration and display, decomposed differently from the predicate. `tests/unit/registry.nix` checks that the first and last address of every registry entry satisfies `isBogon`, and that on both sides of every edge of a registry entry or a carved-out allocation an address is in the registry exactly when `isBogon` holds. Adding a bogon means updating both the registry and the predicate.
